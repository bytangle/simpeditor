“The Weight of Days”

The wind remembers every name,
Of those who walked and dreamed the same,
It carries echoes, low and wide,
Of hopes we held and fears we hide.

The sun still climbs with golden grace,
Though time has worn the morning’s face,
And yet we rise with weary breath,
To dance between the life and death.

The roads are long, the nights are cold,
Our stories written, never told.
A moment missed, a word unsaid—
The ghosts we make, the lives we’ve led.

The clock is blind, but never still,
It bends the light, it breaks the will.
And we, so frail with dreams so vast,
Still chase the future, outrun the past.

A child laughs in morning rain,
Unburdened by the weight of pain.
And in that sound, a truth is spun—
That joy begins where fear is done.

We build with hands, with words, with fire,
And fail, and try, and still aspire.
For what is life if not a climb
Through shadowed hills and sharpened time?

Each step a choice, each choice a stone,
Each silence louder than a moan.
We fight for meaning, scratch for grace,
In this brief flash through endless space.

So love the ones who walk your trail,
Hold fast to them when all else fails.
Forgive the past, it does not speak—
The present is the hill we seek.

Though all will fade, the stars remain,
And light the dark in soft refrain.
So walk with wonder, speak your name—
And leave behind a gentle flame.
